# Visitors

Matomo gives you an overview of your visitors to help you understand the popularity of your site.

With the Visits log and the Visitor Profile, you can get a clearer picture of each visitor's experience at a glance - and in real-time.

## The Visitors Overview report

The Visitors Overview page provides a high-level summary of your website’s visitor numbers and engagement. You can access this page within the Matomo dashboard by clicking the **Visitors** menu item in the main navigation and then clicking on **Overview**.

![Visitors Overview](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_890+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/visitors-overview.png)

The analytics data shown on this page is defined by data selectors at the top of the page. Make sure you take a moment to ensure that you have selected the correct website, date range, and segment that you’d like to analyse.

### Visits Over Time <a href="#visits-over-time" id="visits-over-time"></a>

The top section provides a row evolution chart that enables you to see how visits metrics are trending over time. The default metric displayed for this chart is **Visits**. It is possible to change which metrics are displayed by clicking on the _**line chart icon**_ ![alt\_text](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_50+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/line-chart-icon.png) at the top left of the chart and selecting one or more other metrics to plot. The metrics available on this page are:

* Visits
* Unique Visitors
* Users
* Avg. Visit Duration (in seconds)
* Bounce Rate
* Actions per Visit
* Maximum actions in one visit
* Visits with Conversions
* Pageviews
* Unique Pageviews
* Downloads
* Unique Downloads
* Outlinks
* Unique Outlinks
* Avg. generation time

You can select multiple metrics if you want to compare them. For example, to compare the number of downloads with unique downloads to understand the reach of your downloaded content, or whether the same visitors are downloading files multiple times.

![Visits Over Time](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_890+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/visits-over-time.png)

While working with data in this chart, you can also find all the usual [row evolution options](https://matomo.org/faq/reports/graphs-and-visualisations-in-matomo/#evolution-graph-menu) beneath the chart, which allow things like exporting the data in various formats, as well as changing the reporting period and number of intervals.

### Visits Overview <a href="#visits-overview" id="visits-overview"></a>

The **Visits Overview** section on the second half of the **Overview** page shows a collection of [sparkline summaries](https://matomo.org/faq/reports/graphs-and-visualisations-in-matomo/#sparkline-summary-cards) which provides a snapshot of key visitor metrics over your selected date range.

![alt\_text](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_890+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/visits-overview.png)

The small line chart beside each metric shows a simplified version of the visits over time trend, with a blue spot representing the high point and an orange spot on the low point. Clicking on any of the metrics within this section will automatically update the full evolution graph at the top of the page to allow for deeper analysis of those metrics.

## The Devices report

Matomo offers a collection of **Devices** reports to analyse which types of devices your visitors use to access your site. These reports can be useful for establishing things like whether a user is on their mobile or a desktop computer, which brands they use and how modern the devices are.

### How to analyse Devices reports in Matomo <a href="#how-to-analyse-devices-reports-in-matomo" id="how-to-analyse-devices-reports-in-matomo"></a>

You can access these reports by opening the **Visitors** section of Matomo in the main menu and then clicking on **Devices**. For each of the reports on this page, you can hover your mouse over the individual rows to reveal a relative percentage of your overall visitors. Hovering on a row will also reveal two icons which you can click for more functionality:

* ![Segmented Visitor Log Icon](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_50+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/segmented-visitor-log-icon.png) _**Open the segmented visits log**_ – This loads a visits log for users within your chosen dimension. [Learn more about visits log reports here](https://matomo.org/faq/reports/the-visits-log-report/).
* ![Open Row Evolution Icon](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_50+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/open-row-evolution-icon.png) _**Open Row Evolution**_ – Loads a popup window that enables you to analyse the specific metric over time. [Learn more about row evolution charts here](https://matomo.org/faq/understanding-row-evolution-graphs/).

Additionally, hovering your mouse over any part of the table will also reveal the [standard table options](https://matomo.org/faq/reports/graphs-and-visualisations-in-matomo/#tables) along the bottom of the report. There are five main reports accessible from the Devices section and these are detailed further below.

#### Device type report <a href="#device-type-report" id="device-type-report"></a>

![Device Type Report](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_890+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/device-type-report.png)

Knowing which type of devices your visitors are using to access your website or platform is fundamental for optimising your user experience (UX). At the most basic level, comparing Desktop vs Smartphone usage lets you know whether people are accessing your content or platform on smaller or large devices. However, it also often provides context. Mobile users may be accessing your content while on the go, or multitasking, whereas desktop users may be more focused.

#### Device model report <a href="#device-model-report" id="device-model-report"></a>

![Device Model Report](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_890+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/device-model-report.png)

Knowing the specific model of devices your visitors use can help inform your development strategy. For example, if your users are primarily visiting from Apple iPhones then you will want to make sure that your website is well optimised for the mobile version of Safari. Additionally, if you were planning on developing an app then it would likely make sense to prioritise the Apple App Store.

#### Device brand report <a href="#device-brand-report" id="device-brand-report"></a>

![Device Brand Report](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_890+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/device-brand-report.png)

In a similar fashion to the device model report, the brand report could also influence where you prioritise development resources. If the majority of your users come from the Apple ecosystem, then it would likely make sense to prioritise app development for Apple users.

However, development isn’t the only consideration when looking at the Brands report. Knowing which brands your visitors use can also reveal insights about your users. For example a certain brand may be more popular within specific regions, so if the majority of your users have devices from this brand it may suggest a strong regional influence.

Additionally, the Device brand report can also help influence marketing creatives. For example, if you plan a photoshoot where users will be interacting with your brand through a mobile device, it may make sense to use the brands that your visitors most resonate with.

#### Screen resolution report <a href="#screen-resolution-report" id="screen-resolution-report"></a>

![Screen Resolution Report](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_890+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/screen-resolution-report.png)

Screen resolution reports can be useful for making design and development decisions for your site. If you know the majority of your users access your content with a specific resolution, you will want to make sure that your content and layout is well optimised to display at that level.

#### Configurations report <a href="#configurations-report" id="configurations-report"></a>

You may have noticed in the screenshot for the **Screen resolution** report above, there is also a link to load a **Configurations** report. This adds Operating System and Browser data to the report for even more precise analysis.

![Configurations Report](https://m-img.org/spai/w\_890+q\_lossless+ret\_img+to\_webp/matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/configurations-report.png)

### Using Devices data for segmentation <a href="#using-devices-data-for-segmentation" id="using-devices-data-for-segmentation"></a>

One final consideration is that you can also use the dimensions from these reports to [create segments](https://matomo.org/faq/reporting-tools/build-custom-segments-in-matomo/). Using these dimensions as segments enables you to look at data across the full range of Matomo reports while drilling down into the user experience and flow of visitors using a specific type of device.
